Dr Cathy O'Brien

I explore how digital platforms govern participation and the consequences this has for who is able to build community, belong, and stay safe in digital spaces. My work sits at the intersection of digital anthropology, human-computer interaction, and platform governance, and is grounded in qualitative, participatory, and digital ethnographic methods.

Research

I am a Senior Research Fellow in Human-Computer Interaction at the UCL Department of Computer Science. I completed my DPhil in Cognitive and Anthropological Archaeology at the University of Oxford, funded by the Clarendon Fund. My doctoral research used digital sensory ethnography to examine how people learned pottery skills through digital media during the COVID-19 pandemic, and the impacts this had on their wellbeing. I then joined the Information Security Group at UCL, working on the digital forensic triage in policing project, before moving into online safety and platform governance research through the UseCRT project and beyond.

The core question my research pursues is how people construct individual and collective identities, and build community, belonging, and sociality, through their digital participation, and why some of these spaces flourish as welcoming and sustaining while others become hostile or exclusionary.

At the level of platform governance, I examine the mechanisms through which platforms define acceptable behaviour, construct categories of harm, and establish the norms that govern participation. My work has shown that these categories are not neutral or stable but actively constructed through policy, with real consequences for who is recognised as a victim and who is left unprotected, and for who is able to participate safely in the first place.

At the level of the individual, such as in my work with the pottery community, I am interested in how the affordances and materiality of digital environments shape the social norms, relationships, and identities that emerge within them, including how people present themselves, cultivate belonging, through their engagements with and through platforms.

Projects

Google Gift Grant · UCL

Reimagining Privacy Harm Reporting Using Conversational User Interfaces

Exploring how we might reimagine the reporting of complex privacy harms, through interviews and co-design workshops with practitioners in policing, counselling, and restorative justice.

Google Award · KCL

Scammed by Love: AI-Facilitated Romance Fraud

Cross-national study of AI-facilitated romance scams targeting older adults, combining focus groups, a survey, and community workshops. Collaborating with George Mason University.

REPHRAIN · UCL

UseCRT: Usability of Online Content Reporting Tools

Systematic analysis of 517 policy-related documents across 25 platforms, examining how platforms construct and govern harassment.

UKRI · UCL

Digital Forensic Triage in Policing

Examining how frontline officers triage digital evidence at scene, using co-design and ethnographic methods.

Clarendon Fund · University of Oxford

Potting in the Pandemic: Pottery Skill Development and its Wellbeing impact in Covid-19 Britain

Digital sensory ethnographic study of how people used digital media alongside the tools and materials of pottery to learn craft skills during the pandemic, and the positive and negative wellbeing impacts of this.

Research Associate · 2022

Talking Memory Project

Ashmolean Museum, University of Oxford. Selected as one of five individuals to participate in this nine-month project exploring memory and how museums can support wellbeing in older adults. Developed and delivered a public engagement event integrating object-based research, interdisciplinary theory, and participatory art activities to promote social connection and reflective memory work in museum settings. Pre- and post-session measures showed that participants felt the experience had positively impacted their in-the-moment wellbeing.
